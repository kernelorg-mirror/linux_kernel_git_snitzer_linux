Hi <name>,

We now carry 47.8 GB/s of NFS writes into one E1 port at best (46.0 to
47.8 across runs), 96% of the 49.8 GB/s the wire carries into that port
with no NFS at all, and 60.4 GB/s into both ports together (59.2 to

you for the guidance that got us there. On two ports the limit is our
host's memory, not the card: each copied byte costs about 2.2 bytes of
DRAM traffic, so 60 GB/s is about 131 GB/s of the 166 our four DIMMs can
carry, which is also where a bare memory-to-memory copy tops out here
(62 to 68 GB/s). The way past that for us is not to copy.

This note is about three things we found on the way that belong to the
driver or the card: frames the port's RX FIFO drops and `ethtool -S`
does not show (section 1), and two driver behaviours (section 3). Our
two-port results are in section 2 for information.


1. Frames dropped at the port's RX FIFO, and not counted

The setup: one E1 port (xeu 2.0.1 with our local patches, including
rx_buf_size=8192) on an arm64 server with 16 KiB pages, 4 RX rings of
256 entries, MTU 7792. The client is an x86 machine with a 400G mlx5
port, cabled to the E1 port, writing over NFS on 16 TCP connections,
each with a 1 MiB receive buffer on the server.

What we found. `ethtool -S` on the port lists what each ring was handed
and what was dropped at the ring, and nothing for the port itself. The
SDK built into the module has xsl_rm_eu_rx_port_counters_get(), which
returns the port's RX FIFO counters (struct rm_rx_port_ctr:
tc_drop_packets and tc_drop_bytes, tc_curr_lines and tc_curr_packets
for each traffic class, and fc_curr_lines). xeu does not call it. We
wrote a patch that reads it with the rest of the statistics and lists
those counters in `ethtool -S`. The first read after boot returned
12,015,394 frames dropped in traffic class 0.

With the patch, every frame of a run is accounted for. Two 120-second
runs at 44 GB/s, at an earlier setting of ours that lost more than
today's:

                              run 1           run 2
  client transmitted          719,376,635     694,859,522
  E1 rings: received
    + tail drop + pp drop     719,365,190     694,626,015
  never reached a ring             11,445         233,507
  RX FIFO drops, class 0           13,038         234,332
  ring tail drops                   1,462           7,501
  TCP retransmits (client)         16,326         244,687

Counters were taken at both ends with the link idle before and after;
the ends agree to within about 100 frames while idle. On the client we
used the port's physical transmit count (mlx5 tx_packets_phy);
rx_hw_mru_exceed_packets, rx_hw_other_packets and rx_sw_dropped were 0
throughout. Nothing else loses these frames: rx_packets is the
hardware's count of frames forwarded to the ring (fwd_packets from
xsl_rm_eu_rx_ring_stats_get()), the server's stack drops nothing
(receive queue, backlog, out-of-order and zero-window drop counters 0,
no checksum errors), the client's retransmissions fill real holes (8
DSACKs in a run), the client's port shows no transmit drops, and
`xsllink get --fec-hist` shows no uncorrectable codeword on either E1
port.

At today's setting the loss is small but still there: 4,334 and 4,717
FIFO drops in a 30-second window at 46.2 and 46.5 GB/s, about one frame
in 100,000; the rings dropped 0 and 5, and the client retransmitted
4,341 and 4,761.

How the FIFO behaves. It holds 2 MiB for this port's one traffic class
(16384 lines of 128 bytes, as eu_rx_fifos_init() sizes it and as its
fill count shows): some 268 of our frames, 46 microseconds of traffic
at this rate. Its fill, read once a second through 57 seconds of load,
was 0 in more than half of the readings and under 1,100 lines in 90%
of them, with peaks of 10,915 and 14,269. So it is nearly empty most
of the time and overflows in bursts. The bursts are the sender's: when
the client paces each connection (fq, maxrate 23 Gbit/s a flow, so the
sixteen together cannot offer more than 368 Gbit/s) the FIFO never
holds more than one line and nothing is dropped anywhere, at 37 to
38 GB/s, with the client's connections limited by the receive window
86 to 91% of the time.

Our questions:

a. Is our reading right: the frames are dropped at the port's RX FIFO
   when a burst from the 16 connections exceeds what the packet
   processor moves to the rings in the time the 2 MiB lasts? What rate
   can it sustain from that FIFO to four rings, and is 2 MiB the whole
   of the buffering ahead of the rings for a port with one traffic
   class?
b. We could not get the port to send pause frames. With
   `ethtool -A <port> tx on` (which xeu passes to xsl_rm_eu_fc_set()
   with xoff at 12288 lines and xon at 10240) and the client set to
   obey pause, the FIFO's fill passed xoff as before, it went on
   dropping, and the client's port counted no pause frame received in
   four runs. With `rx on tx on` and the port taken down and up, the
   rings stopped tail-dropping and the FIFO dropped everything instead
   (1.36 million frames in a run), still with no pause frame on the
   wire. What else has to be set for the port to send pause?


2. Both ports at once (for information)

Each port is fed from its own 400G mlx5 port on the same client, 16
TCP connections to each. The card carries both at line rate; what
limits NFS on two ports is our host.

                                          one port    both ports
  plain TCP, receivers discard
    without copying                       49.8 GB/s   98.0 to 99.6 GB/s
  plain TCP, receivers copy into a
    small buffer                          49.8 GB/s   83 to 90 GB/s
  NFS writes, copied once and written
    to NVMe drives                        47.8 GB/s   60.4 GB/s

Each figure is the best run of its kind; plain TCP is measured from
the rings' byte counts, NFS is the client's write rate. In the
two-port NFS runs neither port's RX FIFO drops a frame (0 in 50 runs
on two kernels). That took a different setting from one port's: with
one port's settings on both (a 1 MiB receive buffer per connection,
rings of 256) the two ports carried 43 GB/s between them and each
port's FIFO dropped about 870 thousand frames in 70 seconds, at
21.6 GB/s a port; with a 384 KiB receive buffer and rings of 128 they
carry 60 GB/s with no FIFO drop. That is the same burst behaviour as
section 1, seen from the other side: smaller windows, no bursts, no
loss.


3. Two driver behaviours

a. Deferring interrupts stops the port. With the kernel's per-device
   napi_defer_hard_irqs set to 2 and gro_flush_timeout to 50000 ns on
   the port under load, the rings' frame counts stop within two
   seconds (from 3 million frames a second to a handful) and the
   client cannot reach the server over the port. Writing 0 to both
   does not bring it back; taking the port down and up does. Nothing
   is logged. Reproduced twice, on two kernels (7.1.13 and 7.3-rc7).
   Is xeu expected to work with the kernel's interrupt deferral, and
   if not, what does the card offer to hold interrupts back?

b. Every port's ring interrupts go on the same CPUs. In
   xeu_setup_rings_irq() the Rx ring i gets CPU (2i) % num_online_cpus
   and the Tx ring i gets CPU (2i + 1) % num_online_cpus, whichever
   port it is, and irq_set_affinity_hint() on current kernels sets the
   affinity as well as the hint. So with four rings both ports'
   interrupts land on CPUs 0 to 7, and return there each time a port is
   opened: after a down and up, and after `ethtool -G <port> rx <n>`.
   We move the second port's to eight other CPUs and have to do it
   again after each. With both ports' interrupts on 0 to 7 the two
   ports carried 65 GB/s of plain TCP, where they carry 83 to 90 with
   them apart. Could xeu spread ports over different CPUs (by NUMA node
   and port, as cpumask_local_spread() does), and set only the hint
   (irq_update_affinity_hint()) so that a placement survives?

c. The Toeplitz hash. We have exposed the RSS table, key and hash
   function through ethtool, on top of xsl_rm_reta_set/get,
   xsl_rm_rss_hash_set/get and xsl_rm_rss_hash_type_set(), and place
   each connection on a ring by pointing its table entry there; four
   rings then each carry a quarter to within half a percent. To place
   a flow we read its hash from a received packet, because we cannot
   compute it: with Toeplitz selected the hashes change, and a key
   that repeats every 16 bits gives hashes that repeat every 16 bits,
   but they are not the standard Toeplitz over the IPv4 addresses and
   TCP ports with the key shown, in any field order we tried. Which
   bytes of the 68-byte seed are used for IPv4 TCP, over which fields
   and in what order?

We carry six small patches to xeu 2.0.1 from this work: rx_ip_align
(received data on a 4-byte boundary), a retry so that a ring that ran
empty refills, rx_refill_thresh, rx_buf_size (receive buffers smaller
than a page), the RSS table, key and hash function through ethtool,
and the port's RX counters in `ethtool -S`. We would be glad to send
them if they are of use to you.
